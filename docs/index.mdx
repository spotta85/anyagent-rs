Users have Claude Code, Codex, Grok, opencode, Kiro, and friends on their
machines — each with its own CLI, protocol, and quirks. If you build an app on
top of them, you end up writing a driver per agent.

anyagent is that layer, written once. It finds the agents, speaks each one's
protocol (native or [ACP](https://agentclientprotocol.com)), and gives you one
typed API.

## The whole flow

```text
Runtime::discover()  ->  Runtime::open()  ->  (Session, Events)
```

- **`Runtime`** finds agents and opens sessions.
- **`Session`** is the command handle: prompt, answer, configure, cancel.
- **`Events`** is the stream coming back: text, tool calls, requests, turn ends.

Your app keeps its own transcript, UI, and policy. anyagent owns the processes,
the wires, and the turn rules — so every agent behaves the same way through it.

## Design rules

Three rules keep the interface honest:

1. **Same types for every agent.** Anything provider-specific rides in
   `extensions` instead of leaking into the API.
2. **Capabilities are reported, not assumed.** After `open`, the session tells
   you what this agent can actually do on this connection.
3. **A turn ends exactly once.** Whether the wire said so or anyagent inferred
   it, you get one `TurnEnded`.
